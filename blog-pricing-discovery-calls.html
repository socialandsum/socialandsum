Why your discovery call is where most studios actually lose the deal

Social + Sum · 5 min read

By the time someone books a discovery call, they've already decided your work is good enough. The call rarely gets lost over taste. It gets lost over structure: no clear agenda, no sense of what happens next, and a price conversation that arrives too late or not at all.

The call needs a shape, not just a chat

A discovery call that drifts, however pleasant, leaves the prospect unsure what just happened. The calls that convert tend to follow a simple shape: understand the project and the budget realistically, explain how the studio works and what the next 90 days would look like, and be specific about what a proposal or next step actually involves. Warmth matters, but structure is what turns warmth into a decision.

Say the number range earlier than feels comfortable

Many studios avoid pricing until the very end of the call, hoping enthusiasm builds first. In practice this usually just delays the moment someone realises the project isn't in their range, after they've already invested thirty minutes and started picturing the finished room. Naming a realistic range early filters honestly and protects everyone's time, including the studio's.

End on an action, not a "we'll be in touch"

  • Give a specific next step with a date attached. "You'll have a proposal by Thursday" is a commitment; "I'll follow up soon" is not.
  • Send the follow-up while the call is still fresh. A same-day summary email, even a short one, keeps the momentum that the call itself built.
  • Make the yes easy. A simple way to confirm and pay a deposit removes the friction of "I'll think about it and get back to you," which is often where deals quietly stall.
Worth reviewing: look back at your last few discovery calls and ask where each one actually ended, on a booked next step, or on a vague "I'll follow up." That gap is usually the real leak, not the calls that didn't convert at all.

A discovery call's job isn't to impress. It's to give someone a clear, low-friction reason to say yes while the conversation is still warm.
